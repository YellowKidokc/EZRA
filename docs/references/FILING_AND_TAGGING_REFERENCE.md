# Filing and tagging — scoped reference for EZRA

Based on the working *Canonical Filing and Tagging Standard v0.4* and David's subsequent clarifications. This is a portable subset for the index/resolver build. It does not replace or silently edit the full frozen source standard, settle its open rulings or approve a manuscript.

## Identity and address

Preserve existing IDs and UUIDs. A moved path is an address change; it must not automatically rename or recategorize the document. Existing human IDs such as MIM.06.ACTS remain searchable aliases in this mechanical index. A new hidden UUID does not adopt a new public naming convention.

Do not append zeroes to permanent IDs to represent supporting material. The MIM.00 origin paper and a series-wide 00 operations folder answer different questions.

Logical role companions can be displayed using PAPER_ID::ROLE; Windows filename examples use PAPER_ID__ROLE. Such examples are not claims that files exist and do not authorize renaming them.

## Relationships and standing

Keep physical containment separate from belongs_to, references, supports, depends_on, derived_from, version_of and other typed relationships. Retain source/proposer/review status and relevant source versions. An exact hash proves equal bytes, not that a claim is true.

Processing completion, evidence standing, formal proof, a Lean check, canon and publication are separate. No label or location automatically promotes another field.

Version-bound receipts and source snapshots preserve what was actually checked. A source edit keeps document identity but changes which content is being assessed.

## Review and privacy

A read check means the stated version/scope was read, not necessarily agreed or approved. Preserve findings and history. Unknown stays unknown. Public exports exclude private audits and private location metadata.

No corpus relocation or deletion follows from adopting this technical reference. Actual keeper decisions and unresolved project naming rulings remain outside the initial EZRA build.
